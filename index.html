Creative Fiction

The Forgotten

A short piece written from the unexpected point of view of a discarded blue surgical mask during the early days of the COVID-19 pandemic. Explores isolation, connection, and quiet loss through a single object’s journey.

Read excerpt

“The first day that my struggles began was on January 20th in the year 2020. I accidentally got thrown out of the window of a moving car. As time passed I lay on the ground by the sidewalk… Late at night some man picked me up. I thought it was weird because I smelled terrible and was covered in dirt and rain. I guess the guy didn’t care because he looked the same as me.”

Academic / Satirical Essay

Your Cat Is Trying To Kill You

A playful yet sharp analysis of conspiracy rhetoric, using the exaggerated premise that household cats are plotting against their owners. Written for English 335W, the essay demonstrates understanding of “self-sealing language” and the mechanics of conspiracy argumentation (drawing on Jen Mercieca).

Read excerpt

“The idea of this paper was to make you believe that your cat is a cold-blooded killer… This paper used what I discovered called ‘self-sealing language.’ … In fact, this whole essay is a performance of that very bullshit. I’ve made wild claims, exaggerated cat behavior, drawn connections where there may be none…”

— Full essay available upon request

Academic Essay

Is The Bible Propaganda?

An analytical essay examining how language in different Bible translations (King James, English Standard, and Wycliffe) functions rhetorically. Uses Neil Postman’s framework on propaganda as language that invites immediate emotional response.

Read excerpt

“According to [Postman], ‘…propaganda is language that invites us to respond emotionally, empathetically, more or less immediately, and in either-or-manner’… By changing the wording only slightly between versions, the Bible is able to reach different groups of people with the same core message.”
